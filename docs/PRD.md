# The Daily Ledger: PRD and Build Plan

*Working title · Version 0.1 · 8 November 2026 · Free-tier only*

## 1. Overview

The Daily Ledger is a personalised finance news product presented as an old-style newspaper. A new user picks 5 topics they care about. Every morning the system publishes a daily edition: a front page of the most relevant and most important stories, each written fresh in a concise newspaper tone from facts extracted from the source reporting. Readers can highlight any term for an instant explanation, chat with the article, and rate stories. Those signals train a recommendation system that improves every edition.

**Why it exists:** finance news is dense, jargon-heavy and overwhelming. The Ledger filters it to what matters to you, explains it in plain language, and makes reading it feel like a ritual rather than a feed.

**Portfolio goal:** demonstrate product thinking, a real data pipeline, a measurable ML recommender, LLM engineering under tight cost limits, and exceptional UI craft.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- G1: A distinctive, beautiful vintage-newspaper UI that works well on desktop and mobile.
- G2: A daily personalised edition of 15 to 25 stories, generated once per day and cached.
- G3: A recommender that demonstrably beats simple baselines in offline evaluation, with explainable output (Why am I seeing this?).
- G4: Learn-as-you-read tools: highlight-to-explain, simplify toggle, personal glossary.
- G5: Run entirely on free tiers and free or local models.

**Non-goals (v1)**

- Real-time or breaking-news alerts.
- Trading signals, portfolio tracking, or any personalised financial advice.
- Republishing source articles. We attribute and link out.
- Native mobile apps (responsive web only).
- Monetisation.

## 3. Users

| Persona | Description | Need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curious Beginner | Student or early-career professional who wants to understand finance | Plain-English explanations, no jargon walls |
| Busy Practitioner | Works near finance, has 10 minutes a day | A fast, relevant briefing |
| Hobbyist Investor | Follows markets casually | Depth on chosen topics plus market context |

Primary persona for design decisions: the Curious Beginner.

## 4. User stories

- As a new user, I pick 5 topics in under a minute and immediately see a front page that reflects them.
- As a reader, I receive a new edition every day, dated and numbered like a real paper.
- As a reader, I can highlight a term like basis points and get a short explanation beside the text.
- As a reader, I can switch an article between Original summary, Plain English and ELI5.
- As a reader, I can ask follow-up questions about an article and get answers grounded in it.
- As a reader, I can rate a story and see future editions adapt.
- As a reader, I can see why a story was chosen for me and correct it.
- As a reader, I can browse past editions and review the terms I have learned.

## 6. Functional requirements

### 6.1 Onboarding

- FR-1: Show a grid of 20 to 30 topics styled as newspaper section tags (Markets, Central Banks, Crypto, Startups and VC, Real Estate, Commodities, Personal Finance, Trade and Geopolitics, Earnings, Banking, and so on).
- FR-2: Require exactly 5 selections. Allow editing later in settings.
- FR-3: Optionally ask for a knowledge level (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced), which sets the default simplify mode.

### 6.2 Daily edition

- FR-4: One edition per user per day, generated by the batch job and cached. Opening the site never triggers generation.
- FR-5: Layout: masthead, dateline, lead story, 3 to 4 secondary stories, sections for each chosen topic, a This Day in Markets strip, and a Wild Card slot reserved for exploration.
- FR-6: Each story card shows headline, deck, topic tag, reading time, source attribution and a Why this? control.
- FR-7: Archive of previous editions by date.

### 6.3 Article generation

- FR-8: Per story, extract structured facts from the source text: entities, numbers, dates, short attributed quotes. Source text is held in memory only; only the extracted facts and a short excerpt (max 600 characters) are stored.
- FR-9: Write a 120 to 200 word newspaper-style piece from those facts only (inverted pyramid, short paragraphs).
- FR-10: A verification pass checks every number and named entity in the piece exists in the extracted facts. Failed pieces are retried once, then dropped.
- FR-11: Every story shows Reported by \[source\] and a link to the original. Quotes are limited to a few words.
- FR-12: Where possible, cluster 2 to 3 sources covering the same event and synthesise one story (v1.5).

### 6.4 Reading experience

- FR-13: Highlight a term to reveal an Explain button, then a margin note with a short, context-aware definition.
- FR-14: Explanations are cached by term and context type. A pre-seeded glossary of about 300 terms answers most requests with no model call.
- FR-15: Simplify toggle with three levels, pre-generated or generated lazily and cached.
- FR-16: Terms the user has looked up are saved to a personal glossary.
- FR-17: Ask the Correspondent: a chat panel grounded in the article's extracted facts and text. Suggested question chips, last 4 to 6 turns of history, and 10 questions per user per day.

### 6.5 Feedback

- FR-18: One-tap rating (1 to 5 stars, or a quieter thumbs/flag control on the card), plus Save.
- FR-19: Implicit events logged: impression, click, dwell time, scroll depth, term highlighted, chat question asked, save.
- FR-20: A Show me less like this action that applies a strong negative signal.

## 7. Recommendation system specification

The recommender is the technical centrepiece. It is built in stages so there is always a working system, and each stage is measured against the previous one.

### 7.1 Candidate pool

Each day the pipeline produces roughly 150 to 300 processed stories. Each story has: topic tags, embedding, source quality score, publish time, cluster size (number of sources), and a hot score.

### 7.2 Stage 1: Cold start (topic-based)

`score = topic_match × recency × source_quality`

- topic\_match: 1.0 for a story tagged with one of the user's 5 topics, 0.3 for an adjacent topic, 0 otherwise.
- recency: exponential decay with a half-life of about 18 hours.
- source\_quality: a hand-set weight per source (primary sources such as the Fed and SEC highest).
- Output: top N per topic, with the lead chosen by hot score.

### 7.3 Stage 2: Content-based (embeddings)

- Embed every story with a free local model (bge-small or MiniLM via sentence-transformers). Store in Postgres with pgvector.
- User vector `u` starts as the mean embedding of the 5 chosen topic descriptions, then becomes an exponentially time-decayed weighted sum of embeddings of stories the user interacted with, normalised to unit length.
- Score: cosine similarity between `u` and the story embedding.
- Blended final score: `final = a·similarity + b·topic_match + c·hot + d·recency`, with weights tuned on the offline harness.

### 7.4 Stage 3: Feedback signals

Initial weights, to be tuned offline. Negative events must be recorded, since absence of signal is not dislike.

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5-star rating | +1.0 | Strongest explicit signal |
| 4-star | +0.5 |  |
| 3-star | 0 |  |
| 2-star | -0.5 |  |
| 1-star or Show less | -1.0 |  |
| Save | +0.8 |  |
| Dwell over 60% of expected read time | +0.4 | Expected time = word count / reading speed |
| Scroll depth over 80% | +0.3 |  |
| Term highlighted | +0.2 | Capped per article |
| Chat question asked | +0.3 |  |
| Click only | +0.1 |  |
| Impression with no click | -0.05 | Weak, applied only to top-half slots to limit position bias |

Signal events are stored raw in an events table, and weights are applied at profile-build time so they can be re-tuned without data loss.

### 7.5 Stage 4: Exploration (bandit)

- Reserve about 2 of every 15 slots as exploration slots (the Wild Card section).
- **Method:** Beta-Bernoulli Thompson sampling per user per topic. Reward = read-through or rating of 4 or more. Sample from each topic's posterior and pick stories from the highest samples that the user has not selected.
- Log the propensity (probability of showing each item) at serve time. This enables counterfactual evaluation later.
- Decay posteriors slowly so interests can change.

### 7.6 Re-ranking and diversity

- Apply MMR (maximal marginal relevance) so the page does not contain near-duplicates.
- Guarantee the lead story is high on global hot score, so users still see major news outside their interests.
- Enforce a cap per topic and per source on the front page.

### 7.7 Explainability

Each story stores its top contributing factors, for example: Matches your interest in Central Banks · Similar to 2 stories you rated highly · Top story across 5 sources today · Wild Card pick. These feed the Why this? control.

### 7.8 Evaluation harness

- **Synthetic users:** each has a hidden preference vector over topics and styles, and simulated click, dwell and rating behaviour with noise.
- **Offline metrics:** NDCG@10, Precision@10, Recall@20, coverage, intra-list diversity, and a serendipity measure (relevant items outside the stated topics).
- **Comparisons:** random, popularity, Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3, Stage 4 and the full system. Report as a chart and table in the README.
- **Bandit evaluation:** replay with inverse propensity scoring on logged data, plus simulated regret curves.
- **Real user checks:** once live, track click-through, rating average and day-7 return rate, and compare against the offline results.
- Caveat to state openly in the README: synthetic evaluation measures whether the system learns the preferences it was given, not whether real users will like it.

## 8. Non-functional requirements

- **Cost:** zero. Free-tier LLMs with a fallback chain, local embeddings, free Postgres tier, GitHub Actions for scheduling. Verify current limits before committing, since free tiers change.
- **Performance:** edition page loads in under 2 seconds on a typical connection, because everything is pre-generated.
- **Reliability:** if the daily job fails, the previous edition is served with a clear notice.
- **Accuracy:** zero tolerance for unverified numbers in generated stories. The verification step is mandatory.
- **Copyright and ethics:** no republishing of full articles, source attribution and links on every story, respect robots.txt and terms, prefer public-domain and permissively licensed sources, and a Not financial advice footer.
- **Privacy:** store only what the recommender needs, allow deletion of account and history, and keep event logs free of free-text chat content beyond what is needed for the feature.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG AA contrast on the aged-paper palette, keyboard-reachable highlight-to-explain, and a reduced-motion mode.

## 9. Data model (core tables)

- `users`: id, email, knowledge\_level, created\_at
- `user_topics`: user\_id, topic\_id
- `topics`: id, name, description, embedding
- `sources`: id, name, quality\_score, license\_notes
- `stories`: id, cluster\_id, headline, deck, body, topic\_tags, source\_ids, source\_urls, embedding, hot\_score, published\_at, facts\_json, verified
- `editions`: id, user\_id, date, created\_at
- `edition_items`: edition\_id, story\_id, slot, section, score, factors\_json, propensity, is\_exploration
- `events`: id, user\_id, story\_id, type, value, created\_at
- `user_profiles`: user\_id, vector, topic\_posteriors\_json, updated\_at
- `glossary`: term, context\_type, definition, created\_at
- `user_terms`: user\_id, term, first\_seen\_at
- `chat_messages`: id, user\_id, story\_id, role, content, created\_at

## 10. System architecture

- **Frontend:** Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind plus custom CSS for the newspaper system. Framer Motion for subtle print-in effects.
- **Backend/API:** Next.js route handlers for the web API. The recommender runs in the Python pipeline as batch jobs, so no separate API service is needed.
- **Database:** Postgres with pgvector (Neon or Supabase free tier).
- **Batch pipeline (daily, GitHub Actions cron):** fetch, dedupe, fact extraction, verification, topic tagging, embedding, clustering, hot scoring, then per-user edition assembly.
- **LLM layer:** Gemini free tier primary, Groq and OpenRouter as fallbacks, Ollama for local development. Small models for tagging and verification, a stronger model for writing.
- **Caching:** generated stories, explanations and simplifications are stored once and reused.

## 11. Design requirements

- Blackletter masthead, serif headline and body pairing, small-caps section labels, drop caps, column rules and double-rule borders.
- Paper grain and subtle aging via SVG turbulence, grayscale halftone imagery, and one bold brand accent colour, with occasional muted colour fields (blue, mustard, sage) on selected story cards.
- Margin-note styling for explanations rather than generic modals.
- Graceful single-column mobile layout, A night edition is a v2 stretch goal.
- Deliver a design-token file (colours, fonts, spacing, rules) and a static front-page mockup before any app logic. Story cards come in four variants: lead feature, standard, colour-field and compact brief. Visual references live in design/REFERENCES.md and are inspiration only, not templates to copy.

## 12. Success metrics

**Product (post-launch, small beta)**

- Onboarding completion above 80%.
- Average of at least 3 stories opened per edition.
- Day-7 return rate above 30% among beta users.
- Rating coverage: at least 20% of read stories rated.

**Technical (portfolio)**

- Full system beats the popularity baseline by a clear margin on NDCG@10 in simulation.
- Under 1% of published stories failing the verification check after retry.
- Daily batch completes inside free-tier limits with headroom.
- Highlight-to-explain answers from cache for at least 60% of requests.

## 15. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Free LLM limits throttle the daily job | Batch once, cache everything, fallback chain, cap at 15 to 25 stories per edition |
| Hallucinated numbers in stories | Structured fact extraction plus mandatory verification, drop on failure |
| Copyright concerns | Facts-only rewriting, multi-source synthesis, attribution and links, prefer public-domain sources |
| Too little real data to train the recommender | Synthetic users, content-based cold start, and a small beta of friends |
| Filter bubble | Bandit exploration slots, MMR, and a guaranteed global lead story |
| Scope creep | Strict release tiers; nothing from v2 starts before MVP is live |
| Data source breakage (unofficial APIs) | Prefer RSS and official APIs, wrap each source behind an adapter |
